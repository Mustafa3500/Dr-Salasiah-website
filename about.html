The story

My professional journey began long before Artificial Intelligence became a boardroom conversation.

Over the past 26 years, I have worked across multinational corporations, Malaysian government institutions, GLCs and the property development industry. My career has taken me through organisations including Idemitsu, DRB-HICOM, UMLand and JLand, as well as the public sector at the Prime Minister's Department, Malaysia.

Different industries. Different challenges. But often the same fundamental questions.

Where is the opportunity? What creates value? What makes an investment worthwhile? And how do we turn strategy into something that actually works?

Those questions have shaped the way I approach business, leadership and technology.

My academic journey added another dimension. From Degree on Political Science & International Relations in Deakin University Australia, my doctoral research at Universiti Teknologi Malaysia explored transformational leadership, organisational culture and sustainability, examining how organisations create value beyond short-term performance.

That combination of industry experience and academic research has influenced the way I look at emerging technologies today.

I am interested in Artificial Intelligence, but I am even more interested in what happens after an organisation decides to adopt it.

How does AI work with existing systems? How can businesses use the knowledge and data they already own? What capabilities should organisations develop internally? And where is the measurable return?

Technology can be impressive. But impressive technology alone does not create a competitive business.

The real value comes from connecting technology with strategy, people, operations and commercial opportunities.

That is where I believe the most meaningful conversations about AI should begin.

What I'm doing now.

Today, I lead SOVA Intelligence (M) Sdn Bhd, a Malaysian company focused on AI, intelligent systems, technology integration and industry solutions.

Our work brings together emerging technologies, existing organisational systems and practical business requirements to address operational challenges and create measurable value.

A particular area of interest is technology transfer and localisation — helping organisations access global innovations while developing the knowledge, capabilities and systems needed to use them effectively.

I am equally interested in proprietary data.

As AI becomes increasingly accessible, the competitive advantage may not come from the technology itself, but from the information, experience and operational knowledge that organisations already possess.

Beyond SOVA, I speak at conferences, industry forums, universities and executive engagements on four interconnected themes:

Global AI & Technology Integration
Moving beyond adoption towards meaningful implementation, technology transfer and localisation.
The Proprietary Data Advantage
Turning organisational data and knowledge into intelligence, better decisions and competitive advantage.
AI, Sustainability & Business ROI
Exploring how intelligent technologies can improve efficiency, reduce costs and create sustainable commercial value.
Intelligent Cities & Infrastructure
Connecting technologies, data and infrastructure to improve public services, operational performance and urban resilience.

I also write The Intelligence Brief, sharing perspectives on emerging developments and what they mean for organisations, industries and business decision-makers.

Why this matters now.

AI is changing the business landscape, but access to technology is no longer the biggest differentiator.

More organisations can buy the same AI tools, access similar platforms and experiment with increasingly powerful technologies.

The question is what they do differently with them.

A business can invest in AI without improving productivity. A city can install more sensors without becoming smarter. An organisation can collect enormous amounts of data without turning that information into better decisions.

This is why technology integration, proprietary data and organisational capabilities matter.

The next competitive advantage will belong to organisations that can connect these elements, translate intelligence into action and demonstrate real returns.

For me, the conversation has never been about technology for its own sake.

It is about making technology work for business, people and sustainable growth.
